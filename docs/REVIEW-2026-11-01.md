# Review of redfab, 2026-11-01: five lenses on where the drastic gains are

The state reviewed is 10fae01, placement step 2 committed and proven in the game (`docs/PLAN.md`, "Placement, step 2"): the shipped 23 designs sum to 83,284 blocks (`tests/baselines/area.json`), the five capacity designs to 252,012 (mul4 414 x 117, alu8 413 x 183, counter8 152 x 121, regfile2x4, cpu4 317 x 284 at period 240); adder8 is 250 x 105 in 34 s; cpu4 builds in 1,183 s; the fast `./check` gate takes 66 minutes, `--vanilla` 102. The question put was not the next few percent of area but the drastic improvements, in any direction, and what order to take them in.

How it was made. Five reviewers read `docs/PLAN.md`, the 2026-10-31 review, `docs/DESIGN.md`, the README and the code, each with one purpose and told to overlap: the layout algorithms and the area; the time of a build and of the gate; correctness, adversarially, over the 19 commits since 2ad617c and the model against the game; the structure, asked what now pays negative interest; and the product, as a user who writes a module and wants it in a world. They measured with what exists: the committed logs (`build/m/fold/*.log`, `build/check/SUMMARY`, `build/check/pnr-random.log`), the release `pnr` driver (which reproduces the pre-fold footprints byte for byte), the debug CLI, `sample` on a running build, probe binaries built against `redfab-core` outside the tree, and the browser's own pipeline under Node. Nothing under the tree changed and nothing ran in the game. The synthesis below checked the claims the ranking rests on against the logs and the reviewers' scratch output (the gate's seconds, the seed-7 netlist, the stretched mul4 runs, the shipped sizes); everything else is as the reviewers measured or argued it, marked as such.

## What the five lenses agree on

Four findings came up in two or more reviews independently, with the same numbers, and they are what to do first.

1. **Three quarters of a build is spent, in series, on candidates that lose.** cpu4's six candidates take 306, 182, 133, 173, 166 and 172 s and the first wins; alu8's two `levels` candidates (375 s, 57 %) lost by 17 to 19 % but were never given up, because the floor bounds depth only to the lowest placed row and the router laid nine rows below it; mul4's four are within 2 % of each other, so no bound can prune them. The candidates are independent given their placer; the only coupling, the footprint bound, is sound under any "best completed so far" value, and the pick sorts by precedence, not completion order. Threads in the native build, with per-candidate log buffers replayed in order, give byte-identical design files and take cpu4 from 20 to about 6 to 8 minutes, alu8 11 to about 5, mul4 7 to about 3, adder8 34 to about 22 s. The browser stays sequential (no threads without the headers a static host cannot set); it is served by findings 3 and 4 instead. (Time, area and structure lenses.)

2. **The gate's 66 minutes are one netlist and ten others.** `pnr-random` is 3,296 s, 83 % of the fast gate. One 100-gate, 16-input netlist (`check:478`, seed 7) takes 1,432 s, 36 % of the whole gate, under a comment that says a second one "would put this check over a minute"; ten of the 60 seed-1 netlists take 1,577 s, the other 50 take 184 s together. And the check asserts one candidate per netlist: `build_netlist` keeps the best candidate that passes the layout check and ignores the others, so the 30 candidates a 40-cell netlist builds add time, not assurance. Re-tier the seed-7 netlist (the `--vanilla` tier, or 60 gates), add a strict mode in which every completed candidate must pass and every placer must place, keep the 50 small netlists and two or three large ones: about 600 s, thousands of asserted layouts instead of 91, and the check stops growing with every draw. With pnr-random run alongside the rest of the gate (the other fast checks sum to about 11 minutes and depend only on build-designs), the fast gate is about 11 minutes. (Time and structure lenses.)

3. **The placer is a linear scan.** `Netlist::sinks_of` (`netlist.rs:210`) scans every cell and allocates a vector per call; `spans_in` calls it per net per gap per score, and the searched placer's local search scores about 1.5 n² orders per stage per pass. In a CPU sample of cpu4's placer phase it is 64 % of samples, malloc and free another 20 %. A sink table built once per `Frame` is a half-day, byte-identical change that cuts the searched placers three to five times: adder8 -8 to -10 s of 34, cpu4 and alu8 -35 to -40 s, the browser's adder8 from 34.7 s toward 26 against its 45 s assert. The same scan is what a user sees as 113 s of "the searched rows promise" before a 142-cell ALU routes anything in the debug CLI. (Time, structure and product lenses.)

4. **The next thing the plan names, a bound for the clocked candidates by their shortest period, cannot prune anything as described.** cpu4 kept 317 x 284 at period 240, 21.6 M; its worst loser is 341 x 480. A period floor from the netlist alone (setup over the longest gate path) is about 30 ticks, 356 x 327 x 30 = 3.5 M, nowhere near the kept product; even a placement-aware floor with one repeater tick per 15 blocks of Manhattan length comes to about 70, still no prune. The period is set by routing detours and the input window, which no cheap floor sees. The area lens's version (the Manhattan floor applied to cpu4's pitch4 pair, floors 341 x 480 and 362 x 498, 338 s) prunes only if the floor reaches 120 to 132 ticks: plausible, not certain. The cheap, sound part is a policy: pitch4 has lost every one of the ten clocked designs since the shallow rows, by footprint x period factors of 1.26 (fsm) to 2.6 (cpu4), and has won none; laying it out for a clocked design only when no packing candidate routes saves cpu4 338 s and counter8 15 s and changes no shipped layout. Finding 1 gives the time back whether or not the policy is taken. (Time and area lenses, in disagreement only on the Manhattan floor.)

Behind these, the structure lens names the precondition: the candidate space in `build.rs` is four nested loops, a precedence tuple, a separate lay-out sort key, a `pitch4_given_up` flag set only on draw 0, a `Vec<Option>` kept index-parallel to the candidates, and four name formatters that must agree (`candidate_name`, `draw_text`, the report line, five `Design` fields). Every step commit since 2026-10-31 rewrote 75 to 178 lines of it. Finding 1 wants a `Candidate { style, columns, rows, draw }` with `order()`, an `Objective { Footprint, AreaPeriod }` with `rank()` and `bound()`, a `Bound::admits(floor)` that `place_and_route_bounded` calls, a pure `lay_out(.., &Candidate, &Bound, log) -> Option<Laid>`, and `candidates(netlist)` holding the whole policy; `Design` gets the candidate flattened with the same JSON keys. Two to three days, byte-identical, and the clocked policy, the parallel loop and every later knob become one arm each.

## Area: the directions, ranked by expected gain times confidence over cost

The area lens's accounting, asserted against 27 of the 28 design files: width is 3 per routing column plus 5 per gate column (10 for the flip-flop column) plus 6 for I/O; depth 3 per row, 5 per flip-flop row. The shipped sum is routing 66 %, gate columns 28 %, I/O 6 %; the capacity sum 81 / 17 / 2. The kept candidates route at 1.13 to 2.09 times the channel estimate in shallow rows (adder8 1.50, cpu4 1.74, mul4 2.09, alu8 1.46; weighted 1.35 shipped, 1.71 capacity) and at 1.00 to 1.10 in pitch4 rows, whose 3 x 5 tile has three slots where a 3 x 3 has two. Rows below the last gate are 7.5 % of the shipped area, where the charged draw already runs, and 24.3 % of the capacity area (cpu4 31 of 84 rows), where it does not. Routing tiles are 82 to 88 % occupied in the top third of a design and 51 to 68 % in the bottom third.

### A1. The stretched style above 64 cells: measured today, mul4 -36 to -40 %

`STRETCHED_UP_TO_CELLS = 64` (`build.rs:237`) was set on adder8's loss alone; mul4 and alu8 were never laid out stretched. The release `pnr` with searched rows: mul4 stretched, levels, 284 x 102 = 28,968 blocks against packed 416 x 120 = 49,920 (-42 %) in 11 s against 109; stretched with folded columns (debug `pnr`) 288 x 108 = 31,104 against the shipped 414 x 117 = 48,438 (-36 %). alu8 stretched levels 365 x 204 = 74,460 against the shipped folded 413 x 183 = 75,579 (-1.5 %, a tie); adder8 stretched 290 x 123 (+11 %, as the 2026-10-31 review found) and given up by the floor before routing (290 x 99 > 26,250 kept). The mechanism: mul4's packed gate columns end at rows 7 to 29 of 39 and the bottom third of its routing tiles is 51 % used; the 15-gate columns crowd their stubs in the top rows. Gain: the capacity sum -7 to -8 % (mul4 -17,000 to -19,500 blocks), shipped 0, footprints only (settle unmeasured; mul4's may rise). Cost: a constant, a baseline row, and a capacity run, one day. Confidence high for mul4. Not a shipped-design gain, but the cheapest large number in this review, and the kill test is already done.

### A2. The charged draw above 48 cells

`EXTRA_DRAWS_UP_TO_CELLS = 48` (`build.rs:261`) keeps the third draw off the capacity designs, where the rows below the gates are 24 % of the area. PLAN measured the charge alone on them at -5.5 % (cost 4) and -8.1 % (cost 2): alu8 482 x 183 to 473 x 156, mul4 422 x 117 to 449 x 96, regfile2x4 -6 %, cpu4 -0.6 %, counter8 +4.8 %. As a second draw bounded by the kept footprint it cannot lose area; it costs time (mul4 245 to about 430 s before finding 1). cpu4 is the exception the charge does not touch: its depth is the flip-flop column (16 flip-flop rows, 16 clock inverters, 16 clock feeds: 52 rows) and 36 to 48 nets cross stages 2 to 5 against 10 to 27 free gate-column tiles per column, so its 31 rows below are passage demand (A4, A5 or A3). Half a day; confidence high (measured).

### A3. Q-driven outputs leave westward (2026-10-31 direction 8)

counter4 4 of 4, lfsr3 3 of 3, negedge 3 of 3, counter8 8 of 8 and cpu4 8 of 8 outputs are Q-driven and cross every gate column to reach the output column on the east. The 2026-10-31 review measured counter4 -16 %, lfsr3 -13 %, negedge -14 % with rewritten netlists; new here: these are 8 of the 24 to 48 nets crossing cpu4's and counter8's tall stages, 17 to 33 % of the passage deficit that spills below the gates. About 1.7 % shipped (measured), 3 to 6 % on counter8 and cpu4 (guessed). Cost medium: an output column west of the flip-flop column, a mirrored output tile, `spans_in`, the layout check, `cycles-vanilla`. Kill test half a day (the review's rewritten-netlist `pnr` plus 3 blocks of width).

### A4. The torch at the east port: gate columns three wide, and inverters as wiring tiles

`nor_s` is `ww#>e` and the contract wants dust at the port cell (`cells/README.md:22-25`), which is the only reason `assemble` gives gate columns `PITCH` (`place.rs:852`). `.n./w#>/.s.` is the same dust-block-torch physics with the torch on the port cell (the neighbour's west dust gets 15 instead of 14). By arithmetic, 2 blocks x gate columns x depth: shipped -7,958 blocks (9.9 %: adder8 -2,310, adder4 -882, cmp4 -648), capacity -15,182 (6.0 %); against it the gate-column tiles become 3 x 3 and lose their crossings (adder8 10, cpu4 32, alu8 22) and the repeaters at gate-column turns (adder8 37 of 91, cpu4 48 of 168), so expect net -6 to -8 % shipped, cpu4 uncertain. The same contract clause gives an inline inverter tile (`w#>`, exclusive of the upper slot): fan-out-1 NOTs and the clock inverters leave the gate columns for wiring tiles that also reset strength. Measured today by rewriting netlists so such NOTs become wires, release `pnr`, levels, kept style, pre-fold footprints: mux4 71 x 33 to 62 x 24 (-36 %), param_add 97 x 42 to 96 x 33 (-22 %), signed_sub -15 %, mul4 416 x 120 to 422 x 105 (-11 %), adder4 -11 %, counter4 -10 %, cmp4 -6 %, counter8 unchanged, adder8 323 x 99 to 358 x 105 (+18 %: the estimate promised -34 %, the router needed 86 columns against 74). NOTs are 26 of adder8's 102 cells (22 with fan-out 1), 14 of cmp4's 32, 39 of cpu4's 132 (16 clock); level 1 is NOT-dominated everywhere. As a pick-judged candidate about 3 to 5 % shipped; the depth gain is small (adder8's tallest column 37 to 34 rows by the row estimate, not 33 to 16) because depth is gates plus passing nets. Cost: the contract clause and `validate()`, `nor_sq`/`nor_n`/`not_*` tiles, `Kind::sized`, `col_width`, fixtures, adjacency, `tiles-vanilla`: 2 to 3 days for the width; the inverter 3 to 4 more (a netlist pass marking inverted sinks, placement in `realize_trees` like a repeater with a retry, LVS mapping the torch to the NOT). Risk: the chaotic negotiation; cpu4's repeater sites. Confidence medium for the width, low-medium for the inverter. Kill test, half a day, router only: gate `col_width` 3 and a 3-wide `Kind::Nor` stub with the same ports; `pnr` adder8, adder4, cmp4, cpu4; dead if the routing columns grow by more than two thirds of the gate columns (adder8: more than 7).

### A5. The two-slot tile is the 1.5x: pins and outputs on both levels, or a third level

Every ratio above says the same thing: with three slots per tile the router meets the channel estimate; with two it needs 1.35 to 1.71 times the columns, and routing is 66 to 81 % of the area. Every source is `((col+1,row), Track::X)` and every sink lower-level only (`route.rs:306-352`); a ramp holds all three slots of its tile and costs 2 (`route.rs:72-79`, `1069-1076`); a 3 x 3 tile cannot cross (`route.rs:811-820`). So a stub crossing a trunk on its level needs an exclusive ramp tile, a second passage through a gate column's free row needs the other level and two more ramps, and the router keeps nets single-level, spreads stubs over the top rows and spills passages below the gates. Two forms:

- **Pins on both levels.** An upper output is stone above the torch with dust on it (a torch strongly powers the block above; dust beside a strongly powered block is powered), so `nor` gains an east port on both levels at once; an upper input is the upper port's dust stepping down to a dust lying on the NOR's stone (dust weakly powers the block under it, which turns the torch off and powers no dust). Constraints that fall out: all inputs of one gate on one level (mixed levels short through that dust), and an upper-input gate has a lower-only output. All from Appendix A facts marked MEASURED; the funnel still needs the adjacency check and the game. If routing falls toward 1.1 times the estimate: routing width -19 % shipped (-12 % area), -36 % capacity, plus depth where passages stack: 10 to 25 %, arithmetic on the ratios. Cost 5 to 8 days (four gate variants in two sizes, fixtures, adjacency, `tiles-vanilla`; the router's alternative start and target nodes; a per-gate input level in the placer; the layout check). Confidence medium-low.
- **A third wire level** (2026-10-31 direction 12, re-argued by slot count). Dust at y=5 on stone at y=4 is the proven second-level physics; `HEIGHT` 4 to 6 costs no footprint; three slots in a 3 x 3 restores pitch4's capacity at shallow depth and makes step 4's two trunks per column feasible. 15 to 25 % (guessed). Cost 8 to 12 days (`Slot`/`Track` with six tracks and eight ramps, about twelve tile files in four sizes, `Variant::all` doubled, the adjacency groups about a million, `HEIGHT` through world, structure and playground, `tiles-vanilla`). Risk: 1 to 5 ramps take two tiles. Confidence low.

Two router-only kill tests, half a day each, decide which form if either: for the first, a second start node (upper X) per source and a second target (upper X or Z) per sink, uncoupled, in a scratch `route.rs`; for the second, the level added to `Graph::new` with 3-to-5 ramps exclusive of the upper slot; `pnr` adder8 (packed, searched, folded), cpu4 (stretched), mul4, alu8. Alive if adder8 routes in 52 columns or fewer (of 63) or cpu4 in 72 rows or fewer (of 84). Run both, keep the better, then a coupled variant of the first (the placer alternating gate input levels by row).

### Not re-proposed

The fold of two-gate levels as a second folded candidate: about 560 blocks (0.7 %) for twice the folded time (PLAN). Directions 4 and 11 stay dead: the area lens's own per-level row estimate agrees depth is gates plus passing nets. A 3-deep flip-flop is impossible under the contract (a lock repeater needs its input one interior row from the master: four rows); two flip-flops per row nets cpu4 -2.5 %, counter4 -1 %. A 4-wide pitch has no middle cell for ports; two trunks per column with two levels is dead for the slot reason.

## Time: the findings, ranked

The budget, from `build/check/SUMMARY` (run at 8fdf866): fast gate 3,958 s, of which pnr-random 3,296, build-designs 128, playground-pipeline 83, sim-vs-verilator 77, wasm-identical 49, rust-test 47, live-test 47, build-deterministic 41, cycles-sim 40, build-clocked 37, playground-headless 34, everything else about 100; the 15 server checks 2,134 s of the 6,092. Of a capacity build (release binary, `build/m/fold/*.log`): cpu4 1,183 s = 51 s of searched placers + 272 s of routing attempts without strength retries + 784 s of attempts with retries (269 re-negotiations) + 75 s of check-and-time; alu8 654 = 52 + 233 + 338 + 23; mul4 431 = 15 + 274 + 124 + 17; adder8 34 = 10.5 + 13 + 2 + 0.8 + about 6 of burnout walks and synthesis. CPU samples: in the placer, `sinks_of` 64 % and the allocator 20 %; in the negotiation, the cost closure and tree growth 61 % and `BinaryHeap::pop` 36 %; realization and repeater insertion 2.3 %. The strength retries cost rounds, not realization: after "a signal dies in 10 nets" the contention jumps from 5 to 140 tracks (`present_factor` reset to 0.5, `route.rs:452`) and takes 20 to 50 rounds to settle.

1. **Parallel candidates in the native build** (agreement 1). `std::thread::scope` over placers x draws with an atomic least-footprint bound and per-thread log buffers; `clippy.toml` bans threads in the core, so either the loop lives in the CLI over `candidates()` and `lay_out()` from the structure finding, or the ban is relaxed under `cfg(not(target_arch = "wasm32"))`. 1.5 to 2 days including the test that a parallel build equals the sequential one on all 28 designs. Byte-identical; more CPU, less wall. Confidence high on the saving.
2. **Re-tier and strengthen pnr-random** (agreement 2). Half a day; the strict mode may surface latent failures in non-kept candidates on its first run, which is the point. Then run pnr-random alongside the rest of `check` (a background job and `wait`; the per-check logs exist).
3. **The sink table** (agreement 3). Half a day, byte-identical.
4. **The A* inner loop.** Key the heap on the cost's bit pattern as `u64` (monotonic for non-negative floats) with the node index as tie-break; a dense `[col][row][track]` index in place of `Graph.index: BTreeMap`; a generation-stamped array in place of `sink_cells: BTreeSet`; the three-term history sum precomputed per node per round in the same order. -15 to -25 % of all routing time, native and browser. 1 to 2 days; any change to a comparison or a summation order changes layouts, so each change is gated by the 28-design diff. Confidence medium.
5. **Defer the exact timing model to the kept candidate.** `analyze_circuit` runs `Model::all_pairs` (4ⁿ ordered input pairs, `EXACT_MAX_INPUTS` 10) inside `check_and_time` for every candidate, and the pick's `rank` never reads it (it uses the path bound). adder4 (9 inputs): 2.1 to 2.5 s per candidate, 7 of its 14.3 s; cmp4 0.4 s x 7. Compute it in `finish` for the kept candidate (and the repicked ones): adder4 -27 %, cmp4 -25 %, the browser's adder4 proportionally. Half a day, byte-identical. Confidence high.
6. **A release or `gate` profile: measured, only 1.2x.** Nine seed-1 netlists: debug (opt-level 1) 50.1 s, release 41.8 s; the release divmod is byte-identical to the shipped debug one and `wasm-identical` already proves release-wasm equals debug-native on 15 designs. The code is memory-bound, so the flag is not the lever; take it with finding 2 for about 10 minutes of 66, with `overflow-checks = true` to keep the one `debug_assert!`.
7. **Pitch4 as the clocked fallback only** (agreement 4). A policy; record in PLAN; keep pitch4 as the routing-failure fallback.
8. **Small and free.** `$REDFAB` goes through `cargo run` about 50 times per fast run (10 to 15 s of freshness checks): resolve the binary once.

Argued down. The period floor for the clocked bound (agreement 4). Skipping the swept rows for clocked designs when the searched promise less: mem2's swept rows won by 37 % in footprint x period. A lower strength-retry budget above N cells (`RETRIES_BEFORE_DEEPENING` 20) saves cpu4 about 150 s but deepens more rows (+2W each): layout-changing, a candidate at best.

## Correctness: what was found

Thirty thousand placements over 1,500 random netlists (1 to 36 gates, up to 8 inputs, 0 to 3 flip-flops, every style x columns x rows, 3,601 differing folds) produced no empty side list, no duplicate side, no feed collision other than a same-net share, no westward net, no same-column west read, no overlap, and placed identically twice; 45 random netlists built twice natively gave byte-identical design files; no hash container, clock or file in the core; every `sort_by` on `f64` falls back to the index. The 19 commits hold.

1. **The input-spacing repick cannot see the candidates the footprint bound gave up on (plausible; the precondition confirmed).** `build.rs:370-380`, `429-474`. A combinational candidate whose floor exceeds the kept footprint returns `Ok(None)` and only pitch4 is remembered. When the kept layout's input spacing exceeds 8 the pick is remade over the laid-out candidates plus a re-laid pitch4, keyed on spacing first, under which a dropped candidate with spacing 8 and a larger area would beat everything that remains. Probe over `random_netlist` seeds 300 to 344: two builds repicked, both with non-pitch4 candidates lost to the bound (seed 301: "stretched placement given up at attempt 5: at least 59 x 12 = 708 blocks against the 705 kept", final pick 750 at spacing 8; seed 320: nine lost). Re-laying seed 301's lost candidate without the bound ends at 1,170 blocks, so the shipped outcome happened to be right; the wrong one needs a tight floor. The reference test skips repicked seeds (`build.rs:1783-1786`), so nothing gates it. Fix: remember every candidate that returned `Ok(None)` and lay them all out without a bound when the repick runs. Shipped layouts unchanged today (every combinational row has spacing 8).
2. **The same-tick race order is agreed with the simulator, not the game.** The simulator delivers same-tick updates in a fixed direction order (`sim/mod.rs:174`, `geom.rs:65-69`) and the timed model takes its reader order from a scratch simulator at rest; `docs/mechanics.md` says the game's order follows block position in default mode and is partly random in experiments mode. Two shipped designs carry such races (PLAN "I/O columns": cmp4's `lt` pulse 22 off, 30 on, decided by the order simultaneous inputs are applied; param_add's six-tick pulse, decided by within-net propagation order, the part experiments mode randomises). Settle, best case and input spacing are measured under one order, and `design-vanilla` traces `max(2, min(32, 300 // (settle+2)))` vectors, about five for a settle-50 design against 4,608 walk steps, so the racing steps are essentially never exercised in the game. Cheapest fixture: record the two known racing steps in-game at three placements, both modes, about ten repetitions each, and compare against the simulator under every `due_groups` order; or gate `timing-measure` on order-independence of lamp timings using the shuffle `cycles-sim` already has. This is the one place the game could still contradict the model, and the project's rule is to settle it before building on it.
3. **Repeater priority -3** (output into another repeater's back or side) is in the simulator (`sim/repeater.rs:13, 61-72`) and not the timed model (-2, -1, 0 only). Unreachable today (every tile puts dust at its ports; the flip-flop macro is excluded from the model); a future tile or fixer placing repeaters nose to tail would misorder ticks silently. Guard: assert in `Model::new` that no repeater's front block is a repeater.
4. **Check gaps.** `harness/area_check.py` `MUST_MATCH = ("cells", "placement")` records none of `columns`, `rows`, `seed`, `rows_below_charged`, so a fold or a draw silently ceasing to apply is not "another layout" unless a number worsens, and `compare` passes vacuously for a figure dropped on both sides (settle and period are gated). `wasm-identical` covers `$DESIGNS` and the playground test adds three; adder8, mem2, lfsr3, negedge and init_reg are never compared wasm against native. `timing-tight` runs the default same-tick order only and never on adder8. No end-to-end test of the repick path. `pnrtest::evaluate_is_nor` checks only the zero-gate pass-through. The `check` script itself is sound: `run` takes the function's status, loops `|| return 1`, `refused_with` is self-tested, the mutation count is guarded.

## Structure: what pays negative interest

1. **The candidate space** (see the agreements): `Candidate`, `Objective`, `Bound`, `Laid`, `candidates()`, `lay_out()`, `pick()`; `Design` with the candidate flattened under the same JSON keys. 2 to 3 days, byte-identical; the precondition for the parallel loop and for every knob after it.
2. **Progress is a sentence stream and nine tests parse the sentences.** `Log = &mut dyn FnMut(&str)` through twelve signatures; twenty `log(&format!(..))` sites format eagerly under the no-op logger; tests match `" blocks against the "`, `", seed 1 placement"`, split the summary line by word position, parse `"widening gap"` lines; `area_check` prefix-matches `"repeater at"` in `timing.outputs[].path`; the wasm returns the log whole, so the page shows nothing during a 60 s route. An `Event` enum (`Candidate`, `Attempt`, `Widened`, `Deepened`, `GivenUp`, `Laid`, `Kept`, `Text`) with `Display` as the one place the sentences live, a `Sink` trait implemented for every `FnMut(&str)` so no caller changes, tests collecting `Vec<Event>`; `RouteFailure::Other(String)` split into `Unroutable` and `StrengthExhausted`. 1 to 2 days; also what lets the browser stream progress through an imported function.
3. **"How big is this cell" against `PITCH` in sixteen places.** `.get(i).copied().unwrap_or(PITCH)` at `route.rs:293-294, 1373-1374`, `build.rs:876-877`; `< PITCH` classifications at `route.rs:375, 811-812, 1384-1385, 1415`, `layout.rs:19, 146, 158`, `tile.rs:970, 1003`; `Kind` has 38 variants, 29 of them size-suffixed copies enumerated by hand. With today's code a 4-wide gate column (A4's cousin) classifies as narrow, `Kind::sized(4,5)` returns `WireN`, and `can_carry_repeater` is wrong, silently, until the layout check. A third level touches about 25 sites in five files (`Slot` with `3 * cell + slot` indexing, `holders[base..base+2]`, `Layout.tiles`/`upper` as two named maps, `Level` with two variants, the `WireUp*` family). A `CellSize { width, depth }` with `narrow()`, `shallow()`, `has_arm(edge)`, `crossable()`, `climbs()` on `Placement` and `Layout`; `Kind { base, family }` resolved from a table built off the tile files' own width, depth and port level at `Library::builtin`; `Layout { levels: Vec<BTreeMap<Cell, Placed>> }`. 1 to 2 days for the first two, byte-identical; 2 to 3 more for the level vector; to be done before A4 or A5, not for their own sake.
4. **Derived data recomputed or copied.** `sinks_of` and `drivers()` rebuilt in `score`, `estimate_routing`, `Frame::new`, `route_with`, `clock_input` (finding 3 of the time list is this); every `Candidate` holds two worlds (pre-bake and baked) until the pick, tens of MB per cpu4 candidate; chunk count and footprint computed in `build.rs`, the report and `area_check` separately; `OutputTiming.path` a string the harness parses. One day; `Netlist { sinks, drivers }` built once; `Laid` keeps the baked world only; one `Design::footprint()`/`chunks()`; `path` as `Vec<PathStep>` with the string as `Display`.
5. **The measurement drivers are not in the tree.** The `pnr` and `est` examples that every step's kill test and this review ran live in an uncommitted directory; `redfab layout` takes only `--style` and prints a picture. `redfab pnr MAPPED.json --style --columns --rows --draw [--all] [--json] [--time]` and `redfab est`, 150 lines over the public API once `candidates()` and `lay_out()` exist. Half a day; it takes half a day off every future kill test.
