# Review of redfab's layout, 2026-10-31: where the area could still go

The state reviewed is aa430dc, placement step 1 committed (`docs/PLAN.md`, "Placement, step 1"): the swept and the searched rows both laid out, the router as judge. Summed block-area over the 23 shipped designs 107,538 (`tests/baselines/area.json`); over the five capacity designs 258,065 (mul4 411 x 111, alu8 483 x 165, counter8 172 x 109, regfile2x4 151 x 157, cpu4 303 x 298 = 90,294). adder8 327 x 99 = 32,373 blocks, built in 41.0 s natively (`build/m/final/adder8.log:134`); the playground pipeline asks that it compile in under half the 90 s limit (`tools/playground_test.mjs:218-226`). The question put was the next optimisation: footprint first, settle and period second, build time third.

How it was made. Five lenses (the data in the logs and baselines, the placer, the router, the tiles, the pick) each surveyed the code and the committed builds and named directions; two skeptics then judged each of the top findings for feasibility and gain, reproducing every number they could with `target/release/examples/pnr`, `est`, `redfab layout`, scripts over `build/m/final/*.log`, and scratch binaries built against `redfab-core` outside the tree (copies with knobs, or drivers over the public API). Where a scratch binary was used it reproduced the committed logs byte for byte with the shipped settings before anything was varied. No file under the tree changed and nothing ran in the game. `pnr` places and routes but does not time, so a footprint below is measured where it says so, and settle, period and input spacing are measured only where a skeptic replicated `finish` (the stretched candidate, direction 1) or read a committed log. Everything else is arithmetic on the logs and renders, marked as such.

What the numbers say about the state first, because every direction below is a reading of it. Every row no flip-flop sits in is three blocks deep and every routing column three wide, so depth is the row count and width is 3 per routing column plus 5 per gate, input or output column (adder8: 74 routing + 19 gate + 2 I/O columns = 222 + 105 = 327). Routing columns are 52 to 71 % of every design's width, and the router needs more of them than the channel estimate says: with rows a cell deep (pitch4) it routes at the estimate in one attempt (adder8 72 of 72, alu8 108 of 108, adder4 37 of 37); in shallow rows the swept candidates end at 1.26 times their estimate on average and the searched ones at 1.61, worse than swept in 17 of 18 designs that tried both. The excess sits in the top rows: contended tracks by rows of ten in the kept styles are adder8 69 of 89 in rows 0-9 of 33, divmod 56 of 56 in rows 0-9 of 20, adder4 37 of 41; the routing tiles of the top third of a design are 82 to 94 % occupied against 40 to 58 % in the bottom third (adder8 87 vs 53, divmod 90 vs 40, signed_sub 94 vs 49). The reason is in the tile rules: a 3 x 3 routing tile holds no crossing, every gate pin is on the lower level, and a ramp holds its whole tile, so a stub turning into a pin across a trunk needs a stack with a ramp at each end, and `pack_stage` lays every stage from row 0 (`place.rs:874`), which puts the pins of every short column, and so the stubs, in the same ten rows. The depth, where it is not a gate column, is rows the router laid below the lowest gate: 0 on adder8 and cmp4 (adder8's 33 rows are its 25-gate first column exactly), 2 to 6 wiring-only rows on adder4, divmod, fsm, counter4, mem2, counter8.

## The directions, ranked by expected gain times confidence over cost

### 1. The stretched style as a third candidate for combinational designs, with the pick's fallback fixed and the style gated by cell count

Mechanism. `Style::Stretched` packs the rows and then draws every column shorter than the tallest out over the tallest's rows (`place.rs:560-569`); for a netlist without flip-flops that is all it does (stages, assembly and the router are the same, `place.rs:197-208`, `:722-732`). Spread over the rows, the pins of the short columns no longer share ten rows, so the stubs that turn into them no longer need to stack over one another's trunks. Compaction step 3 measured stretched as a loss on every combinational design (adder8 455 x 195 against 485 x 175, `build.rs:204-212`), but that was at the 5 x 5 pitch, where every routing tile held a crossing and spreading only lengthened spans; since step 3b the routing tile of a shallow row is 3 x 3 and holds none, and the excess over the estimate is exactly the top-row stub congestion above. Two lenses (data, placement) reached this independently.

Evidence, measured with `pnr` (footprint) and a scratch replica of `check_and_time` plus `finish` (settle, input spacing), controls equal to the baseline rows: divmod packed swept 182 x 60 today (routing columns 39 on an estimate of 26); stretched searched 140 x 60 (25 columns, estimate 24, settle 68, input spacing 10) and stretched swept 146 x 60 (settle 62, spacing 8, repeaters 149 against 173). adder4 179 x 63 to 176 x 60 (settle 66 to 64, spacing 8). signed_sub 123 x 42 to 108 x 45 (44 to 42, spacing 8). mux4 81 x 30 to 75 x 30 (24, spacing 8). cmp4 122 x 51 to 113 x 54 (42, spacing 8 on the swept rows, 12 on the searched). nor3 30 x 12 (pitch4 today) to 24 x 12 (settle 10 to 8, spacing 8). Losses, where the pick keeps today's layout: adder8 300 x 123 and 354 x 126 against 327 x 99; fa1 65 x 33 against 68 x 21; hier 85 x 30 and 76 x 30 against 79 x 24; param_add 101 x 42 against 104 x 39; prio3 41 x 18 against 32 x 21; mul2 and rom4 ties. Why adder8 loses: the stretch `row * (tallest - 1) / (bottom - 1)` sends every stage's first gate to row 0 and its last to row 32, so six gates share row 0 and nine share row 32, and the router spills into rows 33-40 (64, 66, 57, 21, 49, 63, 42, 47 of 86 tiles occupied), the eight rows that are its 24 % depth loss; a stretch that staggers the end rows is the cheap variant to try there.

The skeptics. Both reproduced every footprint and found nothing in the tile rules, the router or the pick's structure that blocks the style. One refuted the finding as stated, on two counts, and the other, who did not refute it, raised the same two. (a) "The pick cannot lose" is false in today's code: `build.rs:303-321` demotes a non-pitch4 winner whose input spacing exceeds `MIN_INPUT_SPACING` (8) to the pitch4 candidate, not to the next-best packed one, so divmod's stretched searched layout (spacing 10) would ship pitch4 at 164 x 69 = 11,316 blocks, worse than today's 10,920: "adding Style::Stretched ... as a plain third entry ships divmod at 11,316 instead of today's 10,920: +3.6 %, not -23.1 %". cmp4 hangs on the same hazard by a tie-break (stretched swept and searched both 113 x 54, settle 42; the earlier, swept, wins at spacing 8; had the searched won at 12, cmp4 would fall to pitch4 104 x 90). (b) The cost is not one entry and not "+14 s on adder8": the build lays every packing style out from the swept rows and again from the searched rows when they promise less (`build.rs:246-258`), so adder8 gets two stretched candidates, 13.0 s and 14.2 s plus checks, about +29 s on a 41 s build, over the pipeline's 45 s. A cell-count gate (adder4 is 49 cells, adder8 102) is therefore mandatory. The non-refuting skeptic added that `pnr` runs no timing, so input spacing was the one unmeasured number; the other then measured it, as above.

Expected gain. As literally proposed (third entry, pick unchanged, no gate): about -1,000 blocks net (adder4 -717, signed_sub -306, mux4 -180, cmp4 -120, nor3 -72, divmod +396) and the pipeline check fails on adder8's time. With the fallback fixed and the style gated on about 50 cells: -3,555 blocks, 3.3 % of 107,538 (divmod -2,160 at 146 x 60, adder4 -717, signed_sub -306, mux4 -180, cmp4 -120, nor3 -72), settle equal or shorter on all six, repeaters down on the five mid-size winners, input spacing 8 everywhere; -3,843 if divmod's 140 x 60 at spacing 10 is declared as a regression instead. adder8 and the capacity designs 0.

Cost. Three code changes, small: `build.rs:211` (Stretched in `COMBINATIONAL_STYLES`, gated on `netlist.cells.len()`); `build.rs:303-321` (when the chosen packed candidate's input spacing exceeds 8, drop it and re-rank the remaining eligible candidates, or measure spacing on every eligible packed candidate before `rank()`, 0.3-0.7 s each on the mid-size designs); six baseline rows re-recorded (placement is in `MUST_MATCH`, `harness/area_check.py:44`), DESIGN 4.3 step 7 and the comment at `build.rs:204-212`. Build time +0.2 to +5.5 s per gated design (adder4 worst), 0 above the gate.

First measurement. Make the two code changes, build divmod, adder4, cmp4, signed_sub, mux4, nor3 (each under 15 s) and read `inputs may change every N ticks` from `report.txt` and the kept placement from the log; N = 8 on all six confirms -3,555. The game: no new tile variants, so no new fixtures; `design-vanilla` and `timing-traces` rerun on the six changed layouts, as after any layout change.

### 2. Input, output and constant columns three blocks wide

Mechanism. Every non-routing column is `PITCH` wide (`place.rs:724`), the input and output columns among them, though their tiles need three blocks: `input_s` is `.#---` (air, the probe stone with its lever, port dust), which `.#-` gives with the probe block still off the border; `output_s` is `---L.`, which `-L.` gives; the ties `..T--` become `.T-`. Their column's wiring is the existing narrow family (3 x 5 in a deep row, 3 x 3 in a shallow one). Every design then loses 4 blocks of width.

Evidence. Arithmetic, exact: width is 3 per routing column plus 5 per other column in every design (adder8 74 + 21 columns = 222 + 105 = 327; cpu4 76 routing + 11 gate + 2 flip-flop + 2 I/O), and the depths of the 23 shipped designs sum to 827 blocks (`tests/baselines/area.json`), so 4 x 827 = 3,308 blocks, 3.1 % of the shipped sum; adder8 -396 (1.2 %), cpu4 -1,192 (1.3 %), fa1 68 to 64 (5.9 %). Not reviewed by the skeptics (it was a lower-priority finding of the tiles lens); the arithmetic needs none, the tiles do.

Skeptics' verdicts. None taken. The open point is the tiles: `tile.rs:525` expects north and south ports at `width.min(PITCH) / 2` (the I/O tiles have none), `Kind::sized` (`tile.rs:919-942`) has no 3-wide gate, input, output or constant, and the lever's attachment must stay off the border like the probe block.

Expected gain. -3,308 blocks (3.1 %) on the shipped sum, -4 blocks of width on every design, certain if the tiles validate. Settle unchanged or 2 dust steps shorter per I/O path.

Cost. Small-medium: six to eight tiles (`input`, `output`, `tie_high`, `tie_low` at 3 x 3 and 3 x 5), their rows in `Kind::sized`, the column widths of the source and output columns in `place.rs:724`, fixtures, the adjacency check (it grows with every new width), and `tiles-vanilla` (about 11 minutes in batches, step 3b). The baseline records the 4 blocks on every row.

First measurement. Write the tiles and build; the area gate measures it. The game must prove the new tiles (`tiles-vanilla`) before the layouts ship.

### 3. Lay the candidates out in the order they can win, and stop one that cannot: build time, no layout changes

Mechanism. Every candidate is routed to completion and then checked and timed, pitch4 first (`build.rs:211-212`, `:246-275`); the pick is footprint first for combinational designs and footprint times period for clocked ones (`build.rs:403-412`). Pitch4 has won nothing above 4 cells since compaction step 3 (nor3 and prio3 today), and the input-spacing fallback to it has never fired on a shipped design (`after all` appears in no final log; all 16 combinational rows record spacing 8). An attempt's footprint is known before it routes: width is the sum of the column widths, depth at least the rows down to the lowest placed gate or I/O (counter4: 10 x 5 + 26 x 3 = 128 and 14 x 3 + 4 x 5 = 62 match the world exactly), and attempts only add columns or deepen rows. So: lay the searched rows out first when they are offered (they promise less), then the swept, abandoning a placer when its floor exceeds the best eligible footprint (strict, so ties still route to completion and go to the earlier style as today); lay pitch4 out only when no eligible candidate exists, its own floor (its estimated columns, which attempts never narrow) is below the best, or the fallback needs it. Three findings (pick, router, data lenses) converge on this.

Evidence, read off the committed logs. adder8: pitch4 3.1-6.3 s (321 x 294, loses by 3x); packed swept 12.9-33.2 s over 8 attempts, lost at 357 x 105; its attempt 2 already measured 92 columns = 318 blocks by 35 placed rows = 105 blocks, 33,390 > the searched 32,373; packed searched 33.2-41.0 s, won. cpu4: pitch4 103.8 s of 385.6 (363 x 410, loses by 1.6x); stretched swept 130.5 s, lost at 351 x 327; its attempts 1-6 measured 309-330 blocks wide by 275 deep (16 flip-flop rows x 5 + 65 x 3) = 85,000-90,750 against the searched 90,294. mul4: pitch4 21.6 s, swept packed 120.6 s in 18 attempts, lost; the floor passes the searched 45,621 at attempt 11 (90 s). alu8: swept won, only pitch4's 19.6 s is saved. counter8 pitch4 8.3 s of 13.2.

Skeptics' verdicts. None (lower priority). The conditions a reviewer named must hold: pitch4 stays the fallback when every packing style fails to route (cpu4 needed it before step 3b's deepening), ties route to completion, and for clocked designs the bound needs a period lower bound, so apply the footprint bound to combinational designs only and the lazy pitch4 to both.

Expected gain. Area 0, every `design.json` byte-identical. adder8 41 s to about 25-31 s natively (the headroom direction 1 needs under the 45 s rule if the gate is ever lifted); cpu4 386 to about 175 s; mul4 230 to about 156 s; counter8 -8 s; regfile2x4 -5 s. Ranked third, with no area, because it is the cheapest change on the list, the only one that moves the third objective, and the precondition for any further candidate in the pick.

Cost. Small, `build.rs` only: the candidate order, a footprint-floor helper on `Placement`, an optional bound into `place_and_route_logged`, lazy pitch4 for the two fallbacks, one test that the floor never exceeds a finished candidate's area, DESIGN 4.3 step 7 and a PLAN line.

First measurement. Build the 23 shipped and five capacity designs and compare `design.json` byte for byte (`build-deterministic` and the area gate already do); the per-design wall times from the logs give the saving. No game run: the layouts do not change.

### 4. adder8's depth is its first gate column; split or rebalance the tallest logic level

Mechanism. A gate's column is its logic level (`place.rs:161-208`) and depth is the tallest column: in the adder8 render column 3 (level 1) holds 25 gates (17 NOT, 8 NOR2, all fed by inputs) plus 8 feed and stack tiles in 33 of 33 rows, the next column holds 10 gates, and the columns of stages 6-19 end at rows 9-10; the lowest placed row is 32 and the layout has 33 rows, so there the router adds nothing. The same shape in alu8 (level 1: 27 cells, 18 NOT, against 19 next), counter8 (18, 17 NOT, against 4), adder4 (13, 9 NOT, against 6), counter4 (10 against 4), cpu4 (32, 28 NOT, against 19). 33 of adder8's 102 gates (51 of alu8's, 13 of adder4's) have slack: their sinks sit stages later. Two ways in: a placer-only one, splitting level 1 over two gate columns (+5 gate +3 routing = 8 blocks of width, 2.4 % on adder8) or assigning slack gates to later columns under a column-height target (a crude load balance gives adder8 a tallest column of 8 instead of 25 but raises the crossing-net sum 222 to 297; a span-aware one, moving a gate only when its inputs already reach the later stage, should raise it far less); or a two-level `not2_s` tile holding one inverter per wire level, which halves the inverter rows but needs ports per edge and level, the placer pairing NOTs, the router accepting an upper-level sink (`route.rs:953`) and a game proof.

Evidence. The render counts above (`pnr build/m/final/adder8/mapped.json packed searched render`), the level histograms from `mapped.json`, and the skeptics' independent depth measurements for the refuted depth-score finding: adder8's lowest gate row 32 in both candidates with 33 and 35 layout rows. Stage 3's own need is about 26 rows (10 gates plus 9 free rows for 16 crossing nets), which would become the depth: 33 to about 26 rows before any width change.

Skeptics' verdicts. None taken on this finding; their caution from the neighbouring ones applies: fewer rows leave the router less vertical room (step 4 of the two-wire-levels stage shows it widening gaps back when squeezed), and the estimate must price the extra pass-through nets.

Expected gain. Unknown until measured. adder8 33 to about 24-27 rows is -18 to -27 % of its area if the width holds, break-even at about +25 % width; honest range 0 to -20 % on adder8 and alu8, 0 to -10 % on adder4, param_add, divmod, counter8, 0 elsewhere (cmp4 and the small clocked designs are not first-column bound). As a candidate the pick keeps today's layout when it loses.

Cost. Small-medium for the placer-only variant (a stage assignment with a height target in `place.rs`, a `Placer` variant, one more candidate in `build.rs`, DESIGN 4.3 step 1); large for `not2_s` (port model, placer pairing, router sinks on the upper level, fixtures, `tiles-vanilla`).

First measurement. In `stages()` behind a flag, split level 1 over two columns (or count two consecutive NOTs of a stage as one row in `pack_stage`), rebuild the examples, run `est` on the combinational shipped netlists plus mul4 and alu8 (estimated columns, rows, under a second each), then `pnr` adder8 packed (about 10 s), adder4, counter8, counter4. If adder8 does not land below about 327 x 75 the placer-only version is dead. No game run for the placer-only variant; `tiles-vanilla` for `not2_s`, only if the placer-only measurement says the depth is worth it.

### 5. The rows handed to the router: cap them, or charge for the rows below the gates

Mechanism. `rows.count = floor.max(base_rows).max(bottom + 1)` (`place.rs:559`) with `base_rows = crossing_rows + 1` (`:447-454`), which exceeds every packed bottom, so the placement hands the router `base_rows` rows (adder8 77, counter4 28, mem2 35, cpu4 99) while the gates occupy 33, 13, 21, about 70; the spare rows cost nothing in the router's costs (`route.rs:742` builds nodes for every free cell), so pass-through nets run straight along empty rows below the gates instead of weaving through the free rows the stretched style interleaves for them, and those rows survive `Layout::size`'s trimming (`layout.rs:280-284`) as pure depth. The search cannot see them: `score()` sums `row_depth` over `rows.count`, a constant per design, so the searched rows are optimised for width alone.

Evidence. Rows holding no gate, flip-flop, input or output, counted from the bottom of the kept renders: counter4 5 of 18 (1,920 of 7,936 blocks, 24 %), mem2 6 of 27 (20 %), counter8 6 of 31 (17 %), adder4 3 of 21 (14 %), divmod 3 of 20 (15 %), fsm 2 of 10, negedge 2 of 12, adder8 and cmp4 0. The skeptics of the depth-score finding measured the same thing from the other side: in 10 of 12 swept/searched pairs the lowest placed row is identical and every real depth difference is wiring-only rows the router laid below it (cmp4 searched rows 17-19, fsm 5 such rows against 2, counter4 7 against 5); over the shipped winners adder4 2, divmod 2, fsm 1, counter4 4 rows = 3,942 blocks, 3.7 % of the shipped sum, "an upper bound for a router-side change". The return-pass skeptics saw the same escape: a column taken away sends the router through a free southern row, which on adder8 costs 981 blocks against the column's 297. And the stretched adder8 (direction 1) spills eight rows.

Skeptics' verdicts. The mechanism is confirmed three ways but the remedy is unmeasured, and one dissent is on record: a row cap on adder8 stretched "would push it wider, not to 300 x 99", since the router widened six times with 77 rows available. The depth-score skeptic named the sharper version: a score term `max over stages of (crossing nets - free rows)` as the depth the search could see, after checking that this deficit predicts the overflow.

Expected gain. Unknown. The bound is the rows above (counter4 24 %, mem2 20 %, counter8 17 %, adder4 14 %, divmod 15 %; 3.7 % of the shipped sum over the four shipped winners with overshoot); step 4 of the wire-level stage says the router widens back when squeezed, so a third to a half of the bound on the clocked designs and little on the combinational ones is the honest expectation, and the sign is not certain on any one design.

Cost. Small for the experiment (one constant in `Frame::lay`); small-medium for the real thing (a capped-rows candidate in the pick, or a per-row cost below the lowest gate in the router; then `score()` using the occupied rows so the search can trade width for depth). No tiles; the game check reruns on the changed layouts.

First measurement. Cap `rows.count` at (max bottom + 1 + k) for k in {1, 2, 4}, rebuild the `pnr` example, run counter4, mem2, counter8, adder4, divmod in their kept styles with both row sets (seconds each) against 128 x 62, 109 x 89, 172 x 109, 179 x 63, 182 x 60: if the width grows by less than the depth falls on most of them, add it as a candidate. Separately, correlate the router-only rows with the per-stage deficit (crossing nets minus free rows) to see whether a score term could predict them.

### 6. A score the search can be trusted with: entries and ramps, not only trunks

Mechanism. The estimate (`place.rs:751-822`) counts one north-south trunk per overlapping span. In a shallow row the routing tile is 3 x 3 and holds no crossing (`route.rs:752`), every pin is lower (`route.rs:252-291`), a net stays off the upper level over its own sinks (`:938-954`) and a ramp holds all three slots of its tile (`:77`), so real demand in a gap's last column is spans plus the entries at every pin row plus the driver rows, each entry or level change a whole tile that removes that row from the column's trunk capacity. The local search compresses spans onto the sink rows, which are the entry rows, so it piles trunks where it piles ramps; the swept rows are not optimised and spread both.

Evidence. Ratios of routed to estimated columns from the final logs: pitch4 1.00-1.10; swept packed 1.40, swept stretched 1.15; searched packed 1.70, searched stretched 1.51; searched worse than swept in 17 of 18 designs. The adder4 render pair: swept 134 lower west-east straights, 25 lower north-south, 102 upper-only, 176 stacks, 80 ramps (13 % of occupied routing tiles); searched gap 0 widened 3 to 8 with the same two nets (2, 6) contending each new last column at rows 10-11 (`adder4.log:67,71,76`), and by hand the interleaved entries there need 4-5 columns against the swept order's 2. Of 1,200 contended tracks named in the final logs, 297 lie in a gap's last column (228 plus 69 in single-column gaps), 118 in its first, 293 in the middle, 44 in gate columns: enriched 1.5x in the last column, not dominant.

Skeptics' verdicts. Split. One confirmed the mechanism and the kill test ("the proposed 18-design rank test is the right one-hour kill test"), with three cautions: the terms go in `score()` and the promised/kept gate (`build.rs:253`), not in `estimate_routing`'s starting widths (step 3b measured a uniformly wider start as +5 % area, cmp4 back to pitch4); only in rows whose depth is `SHALLOW`; and the flat +1 per entry overshoots where a trunk can sit lower in the last column (adder4 swept gap 0: 3 estimated, 2 built), so a discount for nets whose span contains no other net's entry row is required. The other refuted the gain: the router already routes both candidates and keeps the smaller, so an estimate that ranks like the router "changes no pick and no block"; its whole effect is time (alu8 143 s, adder4 6.4 s); an area gain needs the search, driven by the new objective, to find rows that beat both current candidates, which nothing measured supports; in the nine designs where the searched rows lost, three losses are depth-only (divmod, fsm, alu8) and 29-62 % depth in five more, which no span term sees (direction 5); the adder4 gap-0 chase is a widening pathology (the same pair re-contends each new, history-free last column), a widening-rule fix, not an estimate fix; and the ceiling under a model that contradicts itself is 7 %.

Expected gain. 0 to 4 % of the shipped sum (one skeptic 2-5 %, the other about 0), unknown until a search under the new objective is built; also fewer routing attempts where searched rows route at their estimate (1-3 attempts against the swept's 8-10 on adder4 and adder8), which is build time. It is the precondition for searching pin sides or I/O rows, which today's estimate would exploit the same way.

Cost. Medium-low in code (a second estimator beside `spans_in`, read off `pin_edges` and `row_depth`, wired into `score()` only; `est.rs` grows a ranking printout), but each calibration try is a shipped build of about 10 minutes, and the baseline is re-recorded when picks change. No tiles, no router change, no game proof beyond the standing gate.

First measurement. Implement behind a flag; run `est` on the nine designs where swept won and check whether the swept rows now score at or below the searched (the router's verdict); if not, dead in an hour. If so, build the eight shipped ones (about 40 s in all) and read `area.json` against 107,538; a ranking agreement by itself proves nothing about area.

### 7. Fold sparse trailing logic levels into shared gate columns (PLAN direction 2)

Mechanism. The tail of a ripple design is a run of 5-block gate columns holding one to three gates (adder8 levels 15-19: 2, 3, 1, 2, 1; divmod 5-11: 2, 1, 2, 2, 1, 1, 1; adder4 7-11: 2, 3, 1, 2, 1; cmp4 6-8: 2, 1, 1; alu8 16-19: 2, 2, 1, 1), each with a gap of one to four routing columns; about 16 % of gate-column tiles hold a gate (adder8 102 of 627) and the router already uses the rest as routing space (40-60 % of their tiles in adder4). Two consecutive levels can share a column without westward routing if the later gate reads the earlier one's output on its north or south feed from the gap east of the column: the net leaves east, turns in the gap, comes back into the feed, which is how the clock inverter drives the flip-flops beside it today (`spans_in` at `place.rs:788` counts a same-stage net in the gap east of its stage; the router's sink for a north or south pin is the feed tile, reachable from any neighbour). Constraints: at most two same-column inputs per gate (N and S), a NOT fed from its own column pays a feed row it did not need, `assign_sides` must forbid West for same-column nets, and the merged gap carries both levels' spans. Two lenses (placement, data) converged.

Evidence. The width accounting above and the renders (adder8 gate columns 14-18 hold gates in rows 0-6 only; adder4's tail columns carry routing in 13, 9, 12, 8, 4 of their 21 tiles). The same-stage U-turn is proven in every clocked shipped design and in the test `the_clock_inverter_sits_beside_the_flip_flops_in_the_packed_styles`.

Skeptics' verdicts. None taken (lower priority). The caution comes from placement step 1 itself: entries from both sides of a gate column double exactly the pin conflicts that made the searched rows lose, so the merged gap may need what the two it replaces needed.

Expected gain. Width before any routing penalty: adder4 -24 of 179 blocks (-13 %), adder8 -24 to -33 of 327 (-7 to -10 %), divmod -32 to -40 of 182 (-18 to -22 %), cpu4 about -32 of 303, cmp4 about -16 of 122; roughly 9,000 blocks or 8 % of the shipped sum if every merged gap needs only the larger of the two it replaces; 5-10 % the honest range for deep designs, 0 for shallow wide ones and for mul4 (no level under four gates). Settle rises a little on folded paths.

Cost. Large: a folding rule in `stages()`, a forbidden side per pin in `pack_stage` and `assign_sides`, `crossing_of_stage` following, tests for the fold and for feeds entered from the east, DESIGN 4.3 steps 1 and 4, baseline re-recorded. No new tile, so no new fixtures; `design-vanilla`, `cycles-vanilla` and `timing-traces` rerun.

First measurement. An afternoon's hack, not committed: in `stages()` fold every level of four gates or fewer into its predecessor while the merged count stays below the tallest level's and no gate reads three same-column nets; force North/South for inputs whose driver shares the stage; `pnr` adder4, divmod and adder8 packed swept against 179 x 63, 182 x 60, 357 x 105. If adder4 does not route or comes out wider, the direction dies cheaply.

### 8. The clocked designs: one clock inverter, and lamps that do not cross the logic

Mechanism. `dfflegalize` leaves one `$_NOT_` per flip-flop on the clock (`synth.rs:20`, no `opt_merge` after), and the placer stacks all N in the two-cell flip-flop column, a 3-deep row each, their nets turning round in gap 1 beside the Q nets (counter4's flip-flop column reads `DGG.D.G.DG.D`: 4 flip-flops, 4 inverters, 13 rows). Merging them into one NOT, or absorbing the inversion into an inverting input tile, removes those rows; and outputs driven straight by Q (counter4 4 of 4, lfsr3 3 of 3, negedge 3 of 3, counter8 8 of 8, cpu4 8 of 8) cross every gate column to the east edge as pass-through nets, each needing a free tile per gate column and a lower west-east track in every 3 x 3 routing tile on the way; a lamp column beside the flip-flop column (+5 blocks of width, no new tile) or a mirrored output tile on the west would stop that.

Evidence. Footprints measured by the pick lens with rewritten mapped netlists, routed stretched, best of searched and swept against today's baseline (not re-checked by the skeptics; periods not measured, `pnr` has no timing): one NOT: mem2 9,701 to 8,240 (103 x 80, -15 %), fsm 2,720 to 2,516 (-7.5 %), regfile2x4 -2 %; lfsr3 +3 %, counter4 +6 %, counter8 +15 % (the fan-out tree moves from gap 0 into gap 1 beside the Qs). No inverter at all: mem2 7,469 (-23 %), regfile2x4 21,016 (-11 %), fsm -7.5 %, lfsr3 -4 %; counter4 +12 %, counter8 +21 %. Q-driven outputs removed (an upper bound, the output column added back): counter4 128 x 62 to 113 x 59 (-16 %), lfsr3 -13 %, negedge -14 %, counter8 0.

Skeptics' verdicts. None taken. The design dependence is the reason the lens asks for candidates in the pick, not blanket changes; the single-inverter clock tree changes skew, which the hold fixer and `pick_period` absorb today (0 hold-fix rounds on every design).

Expected gain. As candidates: one NOT about -1.5 % of the shipped sum (mem2, fsm, regfile2x4), the inverting input tile more (mem2 -23 %, regfile2x4 -11 %); the lamps about -1.7 % (counter4, lfsr3, negedge); both together roughly -4 % of the shipped sum, concentrated in mem2 and counter4. Periods unknown.

Cost. One NOT: small-medium (`opt_merge` in `synth.rs` or a netlist pass, the layout check against the rewritten netlist, one more clocked candidate, doubling clocked build time unless direction 3 lands first; cpu4 has 16 inverters and must be measured). Inverting input tile: medium, with `tiles-vanilla`. The lamps: medium-large (a lamp column kind or a mirrored output tile, placer rows, `spans_in`, the layout check, `design-vanilla` and `cycles-vanilla`).

First measurement. A full build of mem2 and counter4 from the one-NOT netlist (a temporary `opt_merge` in `synth.rs`) for the period and hold slack; if mem2 keeps period 64 or under, the candidate is a clear win there.

### 9. Gate columns four blocks wide

Mechanism. `nor_s` is `ww#>e`: the second west dust is slack, and dust, stone, torch, dust is four blocks. At four, the stone and so the north and south ports and the column's north-south axis sit at x = 1, which needs a 4-wide wiring family (wire, wire_up, ramps at 4 x 3 and 4 x 5, the north-south ramps mirrored). Two losses: no crossing fits 4 x 3 (the bridge needs port, step, bridge, step, port = 5), so the gate columns' crossings (adder8 24 of 627 gate-column cells, counter4 8 of 126) become stacks with exclusive ramps; and the west arm becomes zero-length, so west-entered turns and fan-outs in gate columns lose their repeater (adder8: 41 west turns, 8 west fan-outs, 17 repeaters in gate-column turns) unless the port-cell repeater (direction 10) lands first. The flip-flop column stays 2 x 5.

Expected gain. -19 blocks of adder8's width (-5.8 %), cpu4 -11 (-3.6 %), alu8 and mul4 -3.9 %, minus whatever the lost crossings and repeater places cost in routing columns, unknown. Skeptics: none taken.

Cost. Medium-large: about ten tiles, the parser's mid-x rule, mirrored ramps, `can_carry_repeater` per side, `Kind::sized`, the adjacency check (tiles side by side only of one depth, above one another only of one width: a 4-wide family multiplies the groups), `tiles-vanilla`.

First measurement, router only: set the gate columns' `col_width` to 4 in the `Placement`, make the router treat width 4 as uncrossable and the west arm as zero-length, keep realizing with 5-wide tiles, `pnr` adder8 packed searched and counter4 stretched; dead if the routing columns grow by more than 6 (the 19 blocks saved).

### 10. A repeater on the port cell, so every 3 x 3 turn, fan-out and up-ramp can carry one

Mechanism. In a 3 x 3 tile every arm is zero-length, so only a straight carries a repeater (`layout.rs:18-27`, `cells/README.md`), and when a run of turns kills a signal the build makes whole rows a cell deep (`route.rs:163`, `build.rs:919-925`): +2 blocks by the full width each. A repeater standing on the entry port cell facing inward, its input the neighbour's port dust and its output the centre, gives every shape one (the centre at 15, every exit port 14, one less reach than today's centre repeater in a straight), the lower-entered 3-block ramps too, never a crossing's climbing arm; its sides face the tile's own corners (air), so nothing locks it. The contract changes by one clause (a port is dust or the input face of a repeater), which the adjacency check then proves over `Variant::all`.

Evidence. cpu4's kept layout has 19 rows made deep (`cpu4.log:352`; 26 in the swept candidate), 38 blocks of its 298 depth (12.8 %) and most of its 16 routing attempts (123 s); dying-signal rounds in the final logs: cpu4 47, regfile2x4 27, alu8 18, mul4 17, counter8 12, adder8 12 (all in swept candidates; 0 in the kept adder8). Skeptics: none taken.

Expected gain. cpu4 up to -38 blocks of depth (-12.8 %) if no row needs deepening, and attempts; regfile2x4, counter8, alu8, mul4 fewer forced detours onto the upper level (each two exclusive ramp tiles), width 0-5 %; adder8 about 0 blocks, some seconds. Settle: one dust step less reach per repeater.

Cost. Medium: README clause and `validate()` exception, `Tile::measure`, `can_carry_repeater` true for every shape, `Variant::all` grows, the router's `Dies.stuck` shrinks to crossings and down-ramps, fixtures, `tiles-vanilla`. No placer change.

First measurement, router only: let `can_carry_repeater` return true for every shape in cells three blocks along the entry axis with needs 1 and out 14 at the exit port, `pnr` regfile2x4 and counter8 stretched (seconds) counting `signal dies` rounds and deepened rows, then cpu4 stretched once (minutes). If cpu4 still deepens rows, the remaining killers are crossings and down-ramps and the gain is only build time.

### 11. When one or two tracks stall a gap, deepen the row that lacks the crossing instead of widening the gap

Mechanism. A `Congested` failure widens every gap holding a quarter of the most contended slots by a column (`build.rs:879-900`), 3 x depth blocks (cpu4 894, regfile2x4 471, mem2 267, adder8 297), even when the stall is one tile. The skeptics who refuted the stall-rule finding (below) found what those tail tiles are: of the 44 columns bought by attempts ending with three or fewer contended slots, 14 are the X and Z of one 3 x 3 routing tile wanted by exactly two nets (a crossing where none fits, which needs ramp, stack, ramp = 3 tiles), 3 are gate-column tiles whose capacity no routing column changes, 4 are one slot wanted by three nets, and the same pair re-collides in the freshly added, history-free column after each widening (mux4 nets 2, 5; mem2 23, 24; adder8 net 79 in attempts 2-4; regfile2x4 net 8 in 5, 7, 8). A deepened row gives that tile 3 x 5, where a lower crossing fits: the resource the two nets lack. A row costs 2 x width, a column 3 x depth, so the trade pays where width is under 1.5 times depth (cpu4 606 against 894, regfile2x4 302 against 471) and loses on adder8 (654 against 297).

Evidence. Kept styles with two or fewer contended tracks at the widening: cpu4 7 of 14 widenings (7 columns, 6,258 blocks, 6.9 %, all UpperX), regfile2x4 5 of 10 (2,355, 9.9 %), mem2 3 of 5 (801, 8.3 %), counter8 2 of 2 (5.2 %), mux4 2 of 5 (7.4 %), mul4 2 of 13, adder8 1 of 5 (297, 0.9 %): shipped 1,959 (1.8 %), capacity 10,260 (4.0 %). The 28 widening lines naming a `Gates` slot (cpu4 `Gates{7} row 39 UpperX wanted by nets 8, 42, 81` cost gap 8 a column of 894 blocks).

Skeptics' verdicts. None taken on this remedy; the stall-rule skeptics' geometry supports the diagnosis and one of them named the "deepen or hand the row to the placer" direction as where the columns should go. The stall-rule skeptics also showed that more negotiation rounds do not resolve these tails (below), so this is the remedy left.

Expected gain. Up to 4.0 % of the capacity sum and 1.8 % shipped if every such conflict resolves without area; about a third of that (cpu4 about 2,000 blocks, regfile2x4 850, mem2 300) if each needs one deepened row. Period unknown (a deep row lengthens north-south hops by two blocks).

Cost. Small: in `place_and_route_logged`, when `tracks.len() <= 2` and 2 x width < 3 x depth, set the named rows' depth to `PITCH` as the `Dying` arm does and retry before widening; row depths already vary per row. Attributing `Gates` and `Sources` slots to no gap (as `Outputs` are) is the same edit. No tiles; `cycles-vanilla` reruns.

First measurement. regfile2x4 (26 s, 9.9 % at stake), mem2 (4 s), counter8 (13 s): attempts and footprint x period against 151 x 157 / 96, 109 x 89 / 64, 172 x 109 / 154. Kill if the deepened row does not clear the stall in one attempt or the period grows more than the area falls.

### 12. A third wire level

The only tile-level way to reopen two trunks per routing column (step 4 of the wire-level stage, measured and not taken): with dust at y = 5 on stone at y = 4 a 3 x 3 tile holds three straights (lower trunk, middle stub, upper trunk), height 4 to 6 costs no footprint, and ramps 1-3 and 3-5 are the existing geometry while 1-5 takes five blocks (two exclusive ramp tiles at each end of a y = 5 trunk in a 3 x 3 column). Gain: adder8 10-25 % of width, bounded above by step 4's 33 % and eaten from below by ramps (adder8 already spends 182 of 2,292 tiles on ramps); confidence low. Cost large: the router's `Slot`/`Track` model, tiles in four sizes, `HEIGHT` in world, harness, playground renderer and export, the adjacency check over three-level stacks, `tiles-vanilla` for every variant. First measurement, router only: add the level to the graph with today's costs and `pnr` adder8 packed searched; dead unless the routing columns fall from 74 to 60 or fewer. Skeptics: none taken.

## Measured or argued down

So that none of these is retried blindly; one line each, with the measurement that settled it.

- Longer PathFinder negotiation before a stall (`STALL_ROUNDS` 12 to 40, or 40 when three or fewer slots remain; a history bump; rotating the re-route order). Measured with a scratch copy of `redfab-core` reproducing every committed log at the shipped settings: the shipped designs 1.7 to 2.2 % worse at 40, per-design swings -14 % (mem2) to +24 % (mux4); adder8 -3.7 % only with the uniform 40 at 3.5 times the routing time (12.4 to 43.4 s on the kept candidate), +8.7 % with the adaptive rule; history bump +7 %, rotation +1.2 %. The mechanism was misread: `route.rs:931-935` rips a net up before re-routing it, the present factor saturates at 1e6 by round 31, and the logs' own 100-300-round runs (regfile2x4 attempt 8 at 307 rounds, mul4 pitch4 attempt 3 at 214) never converged. One side lead, unverified: no `present_factor` reset at strength retries measured -3.5 % over 13 kept candidates (regfile2x4 -34 %, mem2 -12 %, divmod -10 %, counter4 -7 %) but adder8 +13.7 %, shipped +3.7 %, two non-kept candidates failed to route; a full build with timing before belief, and only for the clocked designs.
- A stricter widening rule (half of the most contended slots instead of a quarter). Measured a loss everywhere it changed anything: adder8 78 columns in 9 attempts, 339 x 102 (+6.8 %, 20.8 s against 12.2); fa1 +14 %; divmod +1.6 %; signed_sub +2.4 %; adder4 +1.3 %. More attempts accumulate more history, which spreads nets wider.
- The return pass (PLAN direction 3), prototyped twice over the public API. Accepting on column count grows area on 5 of 8 kept packed designs (the router escapes into a free southern row: 981 blocks against a column's 297 on adder8). Accepting on footprint: adder8 -0.9 % (324 x 99, 13 tries, 18.9 s) or -1.8 % if a gap may go under its estimate (321 x 99, 21 tries, 31.4 s), adder4 -1.7 %, mux4 -3.7 % or hier -3.8 %, counter4 -7.0 % and mem2 -6.0 % (area only, period unmeasured); at most 1,719 blocks, 1.6 % of the shipped sum. Six of the seven adder8 gaps the quarter rule widened only in attempt 1 fail one column narrower, so that widening was needed. A command-line option at best; `Routed` would have to carry its `History`.
- A depth term in `score()` from the lowest placed row. Refuted: the lowest placed row is identical for the swept and searched candidates in 10 of 12 measured pairs (set by the tallest stage at about three rows per gate whatever the order) and points the wrong way in adder4 and divmod; every depth difference the finding cited is router overshoot, which is direction 5.
- An estimate that ranks the two candidates as the router does, tested by agreement on 14 of 18 designs. Measures a predictor, not an outcome: the router already judges both, so no pick and no block changes; only the losing candidate's time (alu8 143 s, adder4 6.4 s). Direction 6 keeps the mechanism and changes the test.
- A target-following packing (each stage near its barycenter rows) in place of the stretched candidate. Argued down: stretched's summed spans are longer than packed's on every winner (divmod 157 against 92, cmp4 128 against 103) and its estimated footprint no better on four of five, so "shorter spans" is not what the router rewards; gain unknown, nothing to support the 3-8 % range.
- adder8 at 300 x 99 by capping the stretched layout's rows at the gates' 33. Unsupported: the router widened six times (79 to 86 columns) with 77 rows available and still spilled; a cap would push it wider.
- Two trunks per routing column, a single-level three-block pitch, the packed style for clocked designs, the estimate-only searched placement: PLAN records each as measured and not taken; nothing in this review changes those readings (direction 12 is the only route to two trunks, and it is a third level, not a second trunk).
- Gate-column slots attributed to a routing gap, and early stop when the same nets re-contend the gap just widened. Real (28 widening lines name a `Gates` slot; cpu4's searched attempts 6-16 are 110 s of 386) and folded into direction 11; its area gain alone is design-specific (divmod's searched candidate at gap 1 = 7 would have been 167 x 63, -3.7 %, and still only one candidate).
- A `not2_s` two-level inverter tile: folded into direction 4, to be drawn only if the placer-only measurement shows adder8's depth is worth it.
- The remaining time findings (lay pitch4 out last; bound the widening loop by the best footprint; route the searched rows first): one direction, 3.
